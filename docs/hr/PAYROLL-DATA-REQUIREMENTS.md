## Strategic direction (decided)

Two very different things could be meant by "build this so HR can run payroll":

1. **A full in-house payroll engine** — this app computes net pay, EPF/SOCSO/PCB, and disbursement files itself. Needs essentially all of the MISSING data below built from scratch, plus statutory calculation logic where an error has real legal/financial consequences.
2. **A Payroll Input Package** — a clean, complete, per-employee-per-cycle extract of everything this system can correctly and reliably compute (hours worked, overtime, paid/unpaid leave days, unresolved anomalies), which HR hands off to whatever actually calculates and disburses pay (HR2000 or another external system today).

**Decided: (2), the Payroll Input Package.** Nothing in the current architecture points toward this app being the payroll system of record — `docs/EMPLOYEE-LIFECYCLE-CHECKLIST-ARCHITECTURE.md` explicitly confirms "no payroll engine exists anywhere in this app" and that final settlement / statutory-benefits cessation are manual HR confirmations today. `get_attendance_dashboard_rpc.sql`'s own header comment already frames the Attendance Overview as meant "for payroll prep," not for running payroll itself. Every recommendation below is scoped to (2).

| Overtime hours (hours beyond 8 paid hours in a day, normal working days only) | **EXISTS**                     | `overtime_hours` on `hr_unified_daily_attendance_view.sql`. **Redefined 2026-09-22 to the Employment Act 1955 s.60A rule, replacing the previous "hours worked after 6PM, gated on total hours > 8" formula entirely** — confirmed with HR that the 6PM rule was wrong. Current definition: `max(0, (hours_worked − 1h unpaid lunch) − 8)`, i.e. **hours beyond 8 paid hours per day**.  **Purely duration-based — there is no time-of-day component at all.** `hours_worked` is built from `MAX(scanned_at) − MIN(scanned_at)`, so an early arrival earns overtime exactly like a late departure: someone in at 07:30 and out at 17:30 worked a 10h span = 9h paid = **1h overtime**, even though they left at the usual time. The old rule scored that day as zero, which is a large part of why it was replaced. 8 paid hours corresponds to a 9-hour clock span, because the flat 1-hour unpaid lunch sits inside the punch span rather than being separately punched.  **Flat company-wide, not per-location.** HR confirmed KL's 17:00 finish is company *leniency*, not a shorter contractual day, so both sites owe the same 8 hours before overtime begins. `work_locations.early_leave_time` drives `is_early_leave` only, and no longer feeds the overtime threshold. (This reverses a decision previously recorded under "do not re-litigate" in `WORK-LOCATIONS-ARCHITECTURE.md`; that doc now carries the correction.)  **Retained from the 2026-09-15 pass, still correct:** overtime is forced to 0 whenever `is_weekend`/`is_public_holiday` is true, since Malaysian OT/rest-day convention pays that whole shift at its own premium rate (s.60(3)/s.60D(3)) rather than "normal rate + OT on top". Those hours are carried by `weekend_hours_worked`/`holiday_hours_worked` and the rate-tier columns instead, so the two can never double-count.  See `docs/OVERTIME-WEEKEND-HOLIDAY-CLAIMS-DESIGN.md` for the related, still-open gap: none of this is reconciled against HR's actual (still paper-based) overtime/weekend/holiday approval process. |
| OT classified by normal/rest-day/public-holiday (different statutory multipliers under the Employment Act) — an hours/day-count **estimate**, not a payable amount | **PARTIAL, upgraded 2026-09-15** | Verified directly against Employment Act 1955 ss.60/60A/60D (monthly-rated employee tiers — cross-checked across multiple sources, confirms the same "6 pay codes" HR's real paper overtime form already uses): normal-day OT is a clean **1.5x hourly rate**; rest-day (weekend) pay for the "up to normal hours" portion is a flat day-wage fraction, **not** a continuous hourly multiplier — **0.5 day's wages** if ≤ half normal hours, **1 day's wages** if > half but ≤ normal hours — with hours **beyond** normal hours paid an additional **2x hourly rate**; public holiday work is **2 days' wages** for any work up to normal hours (no half-day sub-tier) plus an additional **3x hourly rate** beyond normal hours. New `hr_unified_daily_attendance_view.sql` columns implement this as a per-day estimate: `normal_hours_threshold` (**a flat 8 paid hours company-wide as of 2026-09-22** — previously location-derived at 7.5h KL / 8h Meru; HR confirmed KL's earlier finish is company leniency, not a shorter contractual day, so both sites owe the same 8 hours), `true_hours_worked`, `rest_day_wage_tier`/`rest_day_excess_hours`, `holiday_wage_tier`/`holiday_excess_hours` — aggregated per period in `get_payroll_period_summary_rpc.sql` (`estimatedRestDayHalfTierDaysCount`/`estimatedRestDayFullTierDaysCount`/`estimatedRestDayExcessHoursTotal`, `estimatedHolidayFullTierDaysCount`/`estimatedHolidayExcessHoursTotal`) and shown on the Payroll Export table/CSV, every label suffixed "(Est.)". **Superseded 2026-09-22:** this row previously described the s.60A columns as "deliberately kept separate from the existing `overtime_hours` column ... to avoid redefining that column a third time". That third redefinition is exactly what happened — `overtime_hours` now *is* the s.60A normal-day calculation (see the row above), which makes the old `estimated_normal_day_ot_hours` column an exact duplicate of it. That column still physically exists on the view (a view column cannot be dropped via `CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW`) but is marked deprecated, is no longer aggregated by the RPC, and its `estimatedNormalDayOtHoursTotal` output plus the matching Payroll Export column/CSV field/KPI card were all removed — the surviving "Overtime Hours (Est.)" figure reports it instead. **Note the knock-on for KL:** because the rest-day/holiday tiers measure against this same threshold, KL's half-day boundary moved 3.75h → 4h and its full-day boundary 7.5h → 8h, so a KL employee working ~3.8h on a rest day now lands in `half_day` where they previously landed in `full_day`. **Still missing, needs HR/payroll sign-off before trusted**: (1) whether the "beyond normal hours" 2x/3x premium is genuinely additive on top of the day-wage base, or replaces it — best-available reading says additive, not confirmed against the literal gazetted text; (2) which rate wins on a day that's both a weekend and a public holiday (both tiers are computed independently, mirroring `is_worked_on_weekend`/`is_worked_on_holiday`'s own existing precedent, deliberately not resolved here); (3) the statutory rate/multiplier-to-RM conversion itself, which needs employee wage data (still entirely missing, see §1) and confirmation of who's even statutorily covered (**automatic entitlement only applies to employees earning ≤RM4,000/month**, or specific manual-labor job categories regardless of wage — a real constraint this app currently has no data to evaluate). This estimate exists purely to reconcile against the real, claims-module-driven "actuals" once `docs/OVERTIME-WEEKEND-HOLIDAY-CLAIMS-DESIGN.md` is built — it is not itself the payable figure. |

## Planned (not built yet): automated email

The app is still in testing, not used company-wide — automatically emailing every employee every week was judged too risky to enable yet, so only the **in-app** side of the automated weekly reminder was built (§7 above). The email side is fully designed here as a ready-to-build blueprint, not live code:

- **Employee-facing**: extend `send_payroll_reconciliation_notifications()` (or add a sibling function) to also compose and queue one consolidated email per flagged employee — reusing the exact composition logic already in `queue_payroll_reconciliation_email_rpc.sql` (glossary-driven sections, per-category deep links, "None — all clear" for empty sections), inserted into `email_queue` per employee with a resolved email. Requires adding a `source text default 'manual' check (source in ('manual','automated'))` column to `payroll_reconciliation_email_sends` first, so manual and automated sends can be told apart and deduped against each other (exact `period_start`/`period_end` equality, a cooldown window such as 6 days).
- **HR digest**: flip `payroll.reconciliation_digest_weekly`'s `notification_rules` row to `channels = array['in_app', 'email']` — a data change, no code change.

## Recommended phasing (once design starts — not part of this doc's scope)

1. **Shipped**: the Payroll Period Summary — per employee, per confirmed cycle: hours worked, days present/absent, total working days/actual days worked, overtime hours, holiday/weekend days+hours worked, paid/unpaid leave days, and every unresolved anomaly flag that must be cleared before HR can trust the cycle's numbers (§7). Available as an on-screen preview and a CSV export from the Payroll Export tab, now against a confirmed calendar-month cycle (§6). **Also shipped**: the row-click reconciliation drilldown with per-flag deep links and single-employee "Send Email" (§7); an automated weekly in-app reminder, split by category, for employees with a portal account; a weekly in-app digest for HR/superadmin; and (2026-09-15) a verified, location-aware statutory rate-tier hours/day-count **estimate** classifying normal-day/rest-day/public-holiday OT into its correct Employment Act tier (§2) — an estimate for reconciliation, not a payable amount.
2. **Needs small, well-scoped additions**: the statutory rate-to-RM conversion itself (now that the correct tier *classification* is shipped — see §2 — turning a tier + hourly wage into a payable amount is the remaining gap, blocked on employee wage data, see §1), a period lock/freeze mechanism, a page-level "Send to All" bulk-email button (the single-employee RPC is already shaped for this as a small follow-up), and the automated-email design (see the appendix above) — designed and ready, deliberately not enabled while the app is still in testing.
3. **Needs HR/payroll sign-off before it can be trusted, not more code**: `is_paid` per leave type, whether late/absence ever produces a deduction, the real pay-cycle cutoff day, and — newly identified with the rate-tier estimate above — whether the "hours beyond normal hours" 2x/3x rest-day/holiday premium is additive on top of the base day-wage tier or replaces it, which rate wins on a day that's both a weekend and a public holiday, and which employees are even statutorily covered at all (automatic Employment Act OT entitlement only applies at ≤RM4,000/month, or specific manual-labor job categories regardless of wage — this app has no wage data to evaluate that today).
4. **Designed, not built — needs the real paper form first**: a digital Overtime/Weekend/Holiday Claims workflow (employee/HR submission + manager/HR approval + a Google-Drive-attached scan of the paper form, reconciled against the system-calculated hours above), including business-trip attendance types (overseas/local) and their daily-allowance rules (doubled on an overseas-trip weekend, replaced by a Replacement Leave grant on a local-trip weekend) — see `docs/OVERTIME-WEEKEND-HOLIDAY-CLAIMS-DESIGN.md` for the draft schema/architecture. Everything in §2 above is observed-only from raw punches today; there is no submission or approval step for overtime/weekend/holiday work, mirroring HR's still-paper-based process.
5. **Designed, not built — a deliberately separate module**: Finance-owned Expense Claims (business trip claims — accommodation/meals/transportation/subsistence/advances — and normal claims — petrol/toll/parking/mileage/etc.) — see `docs/EXPENSE-CLAIMS-DESIGN.md`. Kept intentionally separate from item 4's Overtime/Weekend/Holiday Claims: different owner (Finance, not HR), different data shape (money + receipts, not hours), different downstream consumer (reimbursement, not payroll hours) — the existing (empty) `Claims.jsx`/`ClaimsManagement.jsx` stubs and their nav copy already confirm this is the intended, distinct domain.
6. **Out of scope for this app** (per the decided direction): salary/compensation data, EPF/SOCSO/bank/tax master data, statutory rate tables, net-pay calculation, bank disbursement files. These belong to whatever system already does payroll disbursement today (HR2000 or similar) — this app's job is to hand that system a complete, correct, per-employee attendance/leave summary, not to replace it.
